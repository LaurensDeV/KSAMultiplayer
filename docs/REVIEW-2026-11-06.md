# Review of 2026-11-06: what four passes found and what survived being attacked

Four review passes — races and frame order, jitter and placement, per-frame cost, protocol and
server — each followed by a separate pass whose job was to knock the findings down. Everything below
is what survived, with the verdict the second pass gave it. Nothing here is fixed yet; this is a
list of work, ordered by what it costs a player times how often it happens.

Line numbers are as of commit `0b55566` (v0.8.0). KSA references are to
`../ksa-game-assemblies/current/src`.

**Everything under "Confirmed" was fixed in the commits that follow this file**, including the
remote ones -- a separate decision at the time was to leave those for the ordinary schedule rather
than the same night, and the schedule turned out to be the same day. None of it has been in a game
yet beyond the two-game rig. The "Refuted" section is the part worth keeping afterwards: it is the
list of things not to look at again.

## Confirmed, in the order worth fixing

### 1. Loading your own save while connected uploads that whole save to the shared world

`Universe.DeserializeSave` (KSA `Universe.cs:2443`) calls `DestroyAllVehicles()` at `:2455` and
deserializes the save at `:2486`. Nothing in the mod knows this happened: `Now` is still
`Stage.Playing`, the client is still joined, and `Seen`, `Built`, `Puppets`, `Owned` and `MadeHere`
all hold references to the Vehicle objects that were just destroyed. Those are reference-identity
sets, so every freshly deserialized vehicle is new to them, and on the first frame after the load
`Adopt` claims, defines and sends **every craft in that single-player save** to everybody.

That is the engine of the 0.7.0 incident -- a game claiming craft that are not its own -- reached
by pressing Escape and loading a save.

The same load also calls `Program.RefreshVehiclesInFrame` at `Universe.cs:2495`, a second call site
the mod's prefix does not know about, so `Settle()` runs mid-load. That part is harmless by
comparison: `ToBuild` is capped at one entry a frame, so at most one stray puppet lands in the
player's own save. It is not a race -- `DeserializeSave` opens with `JobSystems.WaitAll()`.

**Fix.** Prefix and postfix `Universe.DeserializeSave`, reusing the `TakeWorld`/`StartPlaying` pair
that already exists: the prefix goes to `Stage.Loading` and clears the bookkeeping, the postfix
refills `Seen` from the world and goes back to `Stage.Playing`. The cheaper and more honest option
is to disconnect on a unilateral save load and say so in the chat, since the shared world cannot
survive one anyway.

### 2. `PlaceAnalytic` mutates a list KSA's workers are walking

`World.cs:533` calls the public `v.SetFlightPlan(...)` from `[StarMapAfterOnFrame]`, which is a
postfix on `Program.OnFrame` -- at which point four job systems have been queued and not waited:
`NearestOrbitAndPerformanceWorker`, `ClothSolvers`, `VehicleSolver` and `OrbitSolvers`.

`SetFlightPlan` (`Vehicle.cs:698-702`) does `Children.Remove(this); Parent.Children.Add(this)`
immediately (`:709-710`) whenever the parent changed, and `IParentBody.Children` is a plain
`List<IOrbiter>` (`IParentBody.cs:27`, `Celestial.cs:22`). That list is walked on the vehicle solver
by `PhysicsStates.CheckSoiTransitions` (`PhysicsStates.cs:723`, from `PhysicsBubble.cs:2345`,
`:2493`, `:2763`, `:2205`) for every vehicle outside its physics radius.

Two things make this more than a theoretical class:

- **KSA treats this exact mutation as unsafe and defers it.** The whole
  `SetFlightPlanUnsynchronized` / `ApplyPendingParentChildrenFix` split exists for this one list:
  the worker stashes the old parent, and the `Children` edit happens only from the serialized
  `UpdateFromTaskResultsSynchronized`. Across the whole decompile, every caller of the public
  `SetFlightPlan` is in the quiet window or on the serialized apply. The mod is the only caller in
  the process that violates that.
- **The list is already in the crash stacks.** `errors.md` records six `Collection was modified` out
  of `PopulateBepuStatesFromKinematic` *and `FullPhysicsConstrainedStep`* -- and
  `FullPhysicsConstrainedStep` is where the `Children` walk at `PhysicsBubble.cs:2345` lives. The
  0.5.1 fix deferred builds and destroys, which cured the `_vehicleStates` enumeration at
  `PhysicsBubble.cs:3008`. It did nothing for `Children`, and `DESIGN.md` admits that fix was never
  confirmed under three-player load.

The same call also tears down an orbit's cached points -- `ReleaseCachedPoints()` returns the array
to `ArrayPool` and nulls the field (`Orbit.cs:1377-1385`) -- and this needs no parent change, so it
happens on every placement. The worker reads the vehicle's live `FlightPlan`
(`VehicleUpdateState.cs:155`) and its `Orbit.CachedPoints` (`:596`) from `RunVehiclePostWorkInner`
(`PhysicsBubble.cs:771-790`), near the end of the solver run. Worse, the mod provokes the read:
`PhysicsBubble.cs:773` re-enters `RecalculateFlightPlan` when `!HasCachedPoints`, and
`ReleaseCachedPoints` is what clears that flag.

The guard does not hold either. `PlaceAnalytic`'s `if (v.HasPhysicsBubble) return false` reads a
field the worker writes in `AssignOrphanVehicles` (`VehicleUpdateTask.cs:272`), the first thing the
solver does -- so a puppet that was bubble-less when the main thread looked can have a bubble by the
time the mod acts.

**Fix.** Queue the orbital branch into `Settle()`, the `RefreshVehiclesInFrame` prefix window, which
sits after all four waits and before all four queues. KSA already defers the *other* branch:
`TeleportToLocation` only appends to `InputEvents.TeleportInputBuffer`, drained at
`Program.cs:2386`. Deferring by one frame costs nothing -- states are already 0.15 s in the past and
`PlaceAnalytic` carries them forward to the craft's own origin time, so the extra frame is absorbed
rather than added. Three traps: drain placements *before* `Settle()`'s `if (ToBuild.Count == 0)
return;`, re-check liveness and bubble state at drain time, and decide whether to carry the sampled
state or re-sample.

`Pads.LayOut()` has the same shape -- `template.Locations.Add` at `Pads.cs:300` against
`ConstraintSim.ResolveLaunchPads` enumerating `bodyTemplate.Locations` at `ConstraintSim.cs:557`,
reached from `PopulateBepuStatesFromKinematic`, the method in the recorded crash. The reason it has
never been seen is not that no bubble exists yet (one does, by frame 1 of the main loop) but that it
runs once per process behind `_laidOutFor` and a `ResolveLaunchPads` cache. It rides along free with
the same fix: call it from the prefix rather than the postfix.

### 3. A positional correction is read back as a kitten's ground speed

This is the unresolved "sometimes kittens are jittering".

`World.Pull` writes `states.Kinematic.VelocityPhys` (`World.cs:564`) from the `Vehicle.PrepareWorker`
prefix. `KittenEva.PrepareWorker` overrides the patched method but calls `base.PrepareWorker`, so the
prefix runs for kittens, before the state is latched. `PhysicsBubble.BuildKittenLocomotionFacts`
then reads that field back as `TangentialSpeedPhys` (`PhysicsBubble.cs:1964`), and KSA uses it to
pick the locomotion mode, the animation clip and the playback rate. There is no clamp in between.

With `PullSeconds = 0.15` the correction is position error times 16:

| offset | correction | what KSA does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 cm | 0.2 m/s | crosses `IdleSpeedThreshold`: flips between the idle and walk clips, and `AnimBlendTime` is also 0.15 s so the blend never finishes |
| 24 cm | 1.6 m/s | saturates the walk clip's playback rate at `PlaybackRateMax` |
| 1.05 m | 7 m/s | reaches `TumbleSpeedGate`: the viewer's kitten trips |
| 5 m | 33 m/s | `SnapM`, above which it teleports instead |

And it sustains itself. A puppet kitten's target tangential velocity is always zero -- the mod never
writes movement inputs, and `PhysicsBubble.cs:1915-1918` zeroes `ThrusterCommandFlags` for a non-Mmu
kitten anyway -- so KSA's traction servo brakes the pulled velocity toward zero every substep with
about 0.08 m/s of authority at 60 fps. For a few centimetres of disagreement **the servo beats the
pull**: the offset never closes, so the same correction is written again next frame, for ever. That
is a kitten walking in place while being shoved and braked at frame rate, and it needs no packet
loss, no clock error and no warp. Around 5 cm the puppet also flaps between asleep and awake, since
that is where `Pull` stops leaving it alone and calls `TakeOffRails()`.

The tumble is the weak part of the story: entering `Tumbling` switches the traction brake off, so
the pull wins and it exits quickly, and the mod overwrites the attitude every frame so the body does
not visibly roll -- what shows is the tumble animation over a held-upright body.

**Fix.** Clamp the magnitude of the correction term in `World.cs:563`, not only the distance at
which it gives way to a snap, and keep it under `IdleSpeedThreshold` for a kitten so a correction can
never read as locomotion. Rockets keep the fast pull. Raise the 5 cm deadband at `World.cs:556` for
kittens too. A clamped pull will not close a small offset for a kitten, because the servo brakes it
-- that is the point: a kitten standing 4 cm out is invisible, a kitten walking in place because of
those 4 cm is what players reported. `SnapM` still handles the large case.

This is also the choke point every other source passes through. Loss, clock bias and warp all arrive
as `off`, and `World.cs:564` is where `off` becomes a locomotion input.

### 4. A reconnect renames a player and locks them out of their own craft

Already in `errors.md`, as the `averageksp 2` and `MrJeranimo 2` of the mass-crash session.

Session identity is the source endpoint (`Server.cs:307`), and `Sync.Connect` always builds a new
`Client` whose socket binds port 0 -- so a reconnect can never reuse its port, and `Admit` runs while
the ghost is still in `_players`. Reaped after `SilentSeconds` (10 s), or `LoadingSilentSeconds`
(120 s) if the ghost had not finished loading, which is what the `reload` path produces: `ReloadAll`
sets `Loaded = false` but leaves `Controlling` set.

Then: `UniqueName` makes them `Bob 2`, `Player.Name` is get-only so it sticks for the session and
reaches `admins.json` via `Admins.Check`; `StartPlaying` fills `Seen` from the handed-over world so
every claim carries `made = false`; `Grant` refuses all of them because the ghost still resolves; and
`Lapse` cannot release the one craft the ghost was controlling, because `owner.Controlling == key`
refreshes `AttendedAt` for ever. The player is told *their own old name* is flying their rocket, and
`World.LetGo` strips control every frame while leaving the camera on the hull.

**Fix.** In `Admit`, before `UniqueName`: if an existing player's name matches case-insensitively and
that player has not been heard from for ~3 s, remove them first. One `if`, no wire change, and
everything downstream is already correct once the ghost is gone. A live client pings once a second,
so 3 s does not evict anybody real. The structural fix is a client-generated session id in `Hello`,
which is a `Wire.Version` bump.

### 5. One failed serialization retires a craft for ever

`Sync.cs:1004` records `m.Shape = shape` *before* calling `Define`, and `Define` swallows exceptions.
So a single transient serialization failure leaves `shape == m.Shape` with no definition ever sent:
the craft's states keep flowing, every other game sees its name in `c.Owners`, and no game can ever
build it. An invisible craft in everybody else's world -- the same divergence 0.8.0 closed.

**Fix.** Move the assignment to after a successful describe.

### 6. A `deop` that reports success and does nothing

`UniqueName` dedupes names with `==` (ordinal) while `Find`, `Admins.Add` and `Admins.Remove` are all
`OrdinalIgnoreCase`, so `laurens` and `Laurens` can share a roster. `Deop` then removes whichever
stored hash matches but clears `p.Admin` on only the player `Find` returns, and the console echoes
what was *typed* rather than what resolved. So it can print "Laurens is no admin now" while the real
admin's session keeps full rights until they rejoin. `Admins.Add` also silently deletes the other
row.

The case mismatch is not really the hole, though. A plainer attack needs no case trickery: join
first under the exact name, the real admin becomes `Name 2`, and `op Name` hands the token to the
squatter. The root cause is that the operator verbs resolve an unauthenticated, self-chosen display
name.

**Fix.** Make `UniqueName` case-insensitive, clear `p.Admin` on every match in `Deop`, and echo the
resolved name. If `op` is not going to take a roster id instead, `DESIGN.md` should say out loud that
a name can be squatted.

### 7. `_bestRtt` is a one-way ratchet with no recovery

`Client.cs:313` updates `ServerOffset` only when `rtt <= _bestRtt * 1.5`, and `_bestRtt` only ever
shrinks, with no ageing and no reset outside `Connect`. One anomalously fast packet permanently
lowers the gate for the session; a path that degrades mid-session -- Wi-Fi roaming, a route change,
server load -- locks the offset out with no way back short of reconnecting. Meanwhile
`ServerUniverse` keeps driving this world's clock from the frozen offset, and the ±10% nudge does not
correct it: it drives the local world *to* the biased estimate, so the bias becomes a real
universe-time offset.

0.15 s of clock error is 1.15 km along-track in LEO and 30 cm for a kitten, which finding 3 then
turns into an animation flip.

**Fix.** Age `_bestRtt`, or accept any sample when none has been accepted for N seconds.

### 8. `Define` pays for a serialization before deciding whether to send it

`SendOwned` samples `World.Shape(v)` every frame and calls `Define` on any change; `Offer` only then
applies `RedescribeEverySeconds`. A shedding rocket changes part count most frames, so the
serialize-and-gzip happens over and over and roughly one result per 2-second window is used --
and because `Settled()` flushes last frame's stash at the top of the frame, what goes out is always
one frame stale.

Measured, not estimated: ~0.2-0.5 ms for a small fragment, ~3-6 ms for a 138-part stack, ~15-25 ms
for a 741-part one, and ~20-40 ms in the worst breakup frames once `Adopt`'s uncapped claiming is
counted. Worth fixing. **Not** the cause of the frame-rate collapse -- that remains the catch-up-warp
loop and KSA's own breakup ceilings already written up in `errors.md`. `CompressionLevel.Optimal` is
zlib level 6, the default, and gzip is only 15-25% of the call.

**Fix.** Check the redescribe window in `SendOwned` before calling `Define`, leaving `m.Shape`
unchanged when skipping so the craft re-describes with its *current* shape when the window opens. Do
**not** add a bare per-frame cap: `Mine`'s constructor already records the current shape, so a
skipped `Define` on the `Adopt` path would never retry and the craft would be invisible to everyone
for ever.

### 9. The server can be stopped remotely

Deferred by decision -- the server is known to a handful of trusted people -- but recorded here. The
deployed container sets no `KSAMP_PASSWORD` and is capped at 512 MB with `restart = unless-stopped`.

- **A 18-byte `Chunk` makes the server allocate 524 KB.** `Transfer.Handle` sizes an `Incoming` from
  the client's own `chunk.Count` (ushort, so up to 65,535 pointers) before any guard, with no bound
  on the count, no cap on concurrent transfers per link, and a 120 s expiry. ~29,000x amplification;
  ~1000 datagrams exhaust the container. No `Loaded` guard on the path. Fix: bound `Count` by what a
  legitimate maximum transfer needs, cap concurrent transfers, allocate lazily.
- **`Hello` has no return-routability check**, so forged source addresses fill all 16 slots in
  renewable 120 s windows with no real attacker address in the log, and each forged session makes the
  server stream the world repeatedly to the spoofed address (`Outgoing.Next` re-queues every chunk
  every 2 s while nothing acknowledges). Needs no modified client. Fix: a stateless cookie (HMAC over
  source address and time) echoed in `Hello`.
- **An `Owners` page can overflow the 8 KB send buffer.** Keys are clipped to 96 *characters* but may
  be 255 *bytes*, so a page throws at 32 entries. `Wire.Write` is called outside `Udp.Send`'s
  try/catch and the throw unwinds past the line advancing `_ownersNext`, so every later tick throws
  at the same place: the server keeps reading and rostering but never sends a clock or pumps a
  transfer again. Needs a modified client and multi-byte names. Fix: fill pages by serialized byte
  budget, not a fixed count.
- **Universe time is accepted on a client's word**, checked only for `IsFinite`. A world snapshot
  with `Universe = -1e308` is persisted with `timeKnown: true`, and after a restart
  `ClockFollow.Speed` returns 0 for everybody -- no world advances, across restarts. Reachable only
  by the player the server asked for a snapshot, so it needs an already-trusted client. Fix: bound
  the time to a sane range on both the `Loaded` and `Blob` paths.

### 10. Smaller, confirmed

- **`Disconnect` leaks the removal queue.** `Sync.cs:342` clears `ToRemove` without destroying what
  is in it, so puppets already queued for removal stand in the world for ever -- exactly the
  undriven, unsweepable debris 0.8.0 was about.
- **A lost `Owners` page leaves last round's map.** `_ownersComing` is reset only on page 0 and never
  cleared after committing, so a round whose page 0 is lost commits the previous round's entries for
  any craft the new round omits. For up to 5 s a craft you took in the last 5 s is yanked out of your
  hands every frame with "someone else is flying X". Self-healing -- `SendOwned`'s first loop
  repopulates `Owned` -- and it needs >40 owned craft plus loss in the same window, so a few times an
  hour on a bad link and never on a LAN. If fixed, use a page cursor: clearing `_ownersComing` on
  commit without one trades a stale map for a *missing* one, which puts two players on one hull.
- **`Track.At` allocates a closure, a delegate and a ~208-byte record per call.** ~1.2 MB/s of gen0
  garbage, under 0.1% of a frame. A tidy-up, not a fix: replace the two `FindLastIndex` calls and the
  `Any` with backwards `for` loops, which is exactly equivalent since `FindLastIndex` already scans
  backwards.

## Refuted, so nobody looks again

- **`Track.At` as a frame cost.** Its premise was that it runs on KSA's vehicle worker.
  `Vehicle.PrepareWorker` runs on the **main** thread -- `grep` finds no worker call site at all --
  and it runs once per frame, not once per sim step. Two passes reached this independently.
- **`Pull` abandoning in-bubble puppets during the catch-up warp.** `dt` is bounded at
  `serverRate/30` identically, an order of magnitude below the 0.25 s gate, because `dtPlayer` is
  capped at `1/MinTargetFrameRate` and the headroom ceiling cancels the rest. Only reachable above
  about 7.5x *player-asked* warp. The frame-rate ceiling added in 0.8.0 closed it.
- **An `Owners` page arriving out of order leaving a map with holes.** Because `_ownersComing` is not
  cleared on commit, a reordered page commits a map that is complete but one round stale. The bug
  masks the reorder.
- **`Prune` nulling every held craft definition.** The comparison sign is the other way: it is
  `Prune(+1e308)` that would, not `Prune(-1e308)`.
- **`Adopt`'s LINQ distance search as a cost.** Zero calls per frame in steady state, since every
  craft is already in `Seen`, and `GetPositionEcl()` is an inlined field read. Microseconds even in a
  breakup. The ugliest code in the review and nearly free.
- **The orbit worker reading `_cachedPoints` as the mechanism for the ArrayPool bug.** There is an
  unmentioned eccentricity gate at `Orbit.cs:2958` plus two UI gates, and the timing window is
  realistically closed. The bug is real but the path is the vehicle solver's, above.

## Comments and docs that are wrong

- `World.cs:547` says "the analytic placing takes it over" when `Pull` gives up. It does not:
  `PlaceOthers` only places craft without a bubble, and `PlaceAnalytic` itself refuses one that has
  a bubble. An in-bubble puppet in that state is driven by nothing at all.
- The comment above `Offer` says KSA remakes the stack under the same name whenever a rocket sheds a
  part. For `Vehicle.Split` it does not -- the host tree is reduced in place and the Vehicle object
  survives, which is also why `World.Shape` detects shedding at all.
- `DESIGN.md` documents neither the reconnect rename nor the fact that a display name can be squatted
  ahead of `op`.
- `../KSACars/docs/KSA-FRAME-ORDER.md` documents two job systems live during a mod hook. There are
  four: `NearestOrbitAndPerformanceWorker`, `ClothSolvers`, `VehicleSolver` and `OrbitSolvers`. The
  doc is also written against an older build -- its `PrepareFrame` is at `Program.cs:2000-2064`,
  today's at `2346-2440`. Worth correcting there, since it is the document this work is steered by.
- Good news from the same reading: `Settle()`'s window sits after **all four** waits and before all
  four queues, so it is genuinely quiet for every job system, not only the two the mod knew about.
  Anything moved into it inherits that.
